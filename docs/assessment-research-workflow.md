# VoteAssist Assessment Research Workflow

## 1. Purpose

This workflow ensures candidate policy assessments are evidence-based, consistent, traceable and politically impartial.

AI may assist with finding and summarising evidence, but it must not independently determine or publish candidate scores.

## 2. Research process

For each candidate-policy pair:

1. Identify the candidate and electorate.
2. Read the exact policy proposition being assessed.
3. Search for relevant candidate-specific statements.
4. Search official party policy where candidate-specific evidence is unavailable.
5. Check relevant parliamentary voting records, where applicable.
6. Cross-check claims with reliable secondary reporting.
7. Record the evidence, source URLs, dates and excerpts.
8. Assign a provisional score using the assessment rubric.
9. Write a rationale explaining how the evidence supports the score.
10. Submit the assessment for independent review.

## 3. Evidence recording

Each evidence record should include:

- Source title
- URL
- Publication date, if available
- Relevant excerpt
- Source type
- Whether the evidence is candidate-specific or party-level

Do not record unsupported summaries as evidence.

## 4. Assessment rules

- Never infer a position from party affiliation alone.
- Party-level evidence must be clearly labelled.
- Do not treat a lack of evidence as neutrality.
- Use null when the position cannot be reliably established.
- Do not force a score when evidence is ambiguous.
- Conflicting evidence must be disclosed in the rationale.
- Prefer recent, directly relevant evidence.
- Assess the proposition as written, not a broader interpretation of the issue.

## 5. AI-assisted research

AI can be used to:

- Find relevant sources.
- Extract statements relevant to a proposition.
- Summarise evidence.
- Identify contradictory statements.
- Suggest a provisional score with reasoning.

AI must not:

- Invent sources or quotations.
- Present party positions as personal candidate positions.
- Fill gaps using assumptions.
- Automatically approve or publish assessments.

All URLs and quotations must be independently checked.

## 6. Review requirements

The reviewer should verify:

- The correct candidate is being assessed.
- Evidence sources actually support the recorded claims.
- Excerpts are accurate and not misleadingly taken out of context.
- The score matches the rubric.
- The rationale is understandable and impartial.
- Contradictory evidence has been considered.
- The assessment meets publication requirements.

Published assessments require at least two evidence sources and approved review.

## 7. Research priorities

Research should prioritise:

1. Evidence that directly establishes the candidate's position.
2. Official and primary sources.
3. Recent evidence.
4. Candidates and policies where evidence is available and useful.

Candidates with insufficient evidence must not be penalised. Their positions should remain unknown rather than fabricated.

## 8. Auditability

Every published assessment should be traceable to its evidence and review.

If evidence changes, assessments may be updated. Changes to published assessments should be re-reviewed, and outdated assessments should not be silently overwritten.
