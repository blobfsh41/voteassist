# VoteAssist Policy Assessment Rubric

## Purpose

This rubric standardises how candidate policy positions are assessed. Scores represent the candidate's evidenced position on a specific proposition, not the assessor's opinion of whether the policy is desirable.

## Scoring scale

| Score | Meaning | Assessment standard |
|---|---|---|
| +2 | Strongly supports | Clear, explicit and substantial support for the proposition |
| +1 | Somewhat supports | Supports the proposition, but with qualifications, limitations or partial commitment |
| 0 | Neutral / mixed | Explicit neutrality, genuinely mixed positions, or balanced support and opposition |
| -1 | Somewhat opposes | Opposition to the proposition, but qualified or partial |
| -2 | Strongly opposes | Clear, explicit and substantial opposition to the proposition |
| null | Insufficient evidence | Available evidence does not reliably establish a position |

## Evidence principles

- Assess what the candidate has actually stated or demonstrated, not what their party is assumed to believe.
- Party policy may be used as evidence, but must not automatically be attributed to an individual candidate.
- Distinguish promises, proposals, personal opinions, party positions and voting records.
- Do not interpret silence or lack of information as neutrality.
- Do not infer a position from unrelated statements.
- Consider the context and date of evidence.
- Where sources conflict, explain the conflict and reduce confidence in the assessment.
- Keep excerpts accurate and contextualised.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prioritise:

1. Direct candidate statements and official candidate policy material.
2. Official party policy, clearly identified as party-level evidence.
3. Parliamentary voting records, where relevant.
4. Reliable journalism and secondary reporting.

## Publication requirements

- Every assessment requires a written rationale.
- Published assessments require at least two evidence sources.
- Assessments must pass validation.
- Assessments must be reviewed and approved before publication.
- The rationale must explain why the evidence supports the score.
- A candidate's position must not be invented where evidence is unavailable.

## Independence

Assessors must score the evidence against the proposition, not against their personal political views or preferences.

VoteAssist is an independent information tool. Assessments must not be written to favour or disadvantage any candidate or political party.
